# ABSTRACT:
Acute-on-chronic diseases are marked by progressive deterioration bifurcated by sudden shifts in the compensatory state. Alcohol-associated hepatitis (AH) is an acute manifestation in patients with chronic alcohol-associated liver disease. Metabolic decompensation is a hallmark of AH. How the dysfunction of multiple metabolic pathways collectively lead to overall decompensation remains poorly understood. We developed a flux-centric, coarse-grained mathematical framework to represent hepatocyte metabolism as a network of metabolic modules operating under a constrained resource.  We define the module-level metabolic flux as the overall rate at which metabolites are processed through the corresponding reactions. The stoichiometry of shared resources determines how the module-level fluxes re-balance upon perturbation to the network. We included four interacting modules: glutathione synthesis, methionine cycle, urea cycle, and fatty acid biosynthesis, which are coupled through shared metabolites. We simulated a range of liver states under varying ethanol intake episodes corresponding to healthy and disease conditions. We examined the impact of corticosteroid treatment on the metabolic network in severe AH cases. Simulation results were consistent with clinical observations including down-regulation of ammonia detoxification, reduced antioxidant capacity due to depletion of mitochondrial glutathione, up-regulated fatty acid biosynthesis, and decreased methylation potential. Relaxing overall resource constraints by modifying the network coupling significantly reduced the consistency with clinical phenotypes. The emergent dynamics of the metabolic network captured the heterogeneity of response to repeated ethanol intake and corticosteroid treatment. Simulated corticosteroid treatment scenarios yielded a 72–82% response rate for severe AH cases consistent with the average clinical response rate of 75%.  The simulations also suggest that the treatment can destabilize severe AH cases with milder metabolic decompensation. Overall, our model-based analysis highlighted how decompensation can emerge from interacting metabolic cycles under resource constraints in AH. This mathematical framework is broadly applicable to characterize the landscape of metabolic decompensation in acute-on-chronic failure scenarios.
